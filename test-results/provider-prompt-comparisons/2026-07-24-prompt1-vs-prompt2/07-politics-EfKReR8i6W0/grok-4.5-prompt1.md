**Overview**

In this interview, Colonel Lawrence Wilkerson (former chief of staff to the US Secretary of State) joins host Glenn to assess the escalating US-involved conflicts with Iran and Russia. Wilkerson portrays Iran as resolute and strategically sophisticated, while criticizing the Trump administration as directionless, reckless, and committing war crimes. He warns of cascading global economic collapse, nuclear proliferation risks, alliance fractures, domestic US political meltdown, and a contaminated military culture, arguing that hatred-driven policies have trapped America in an unwinnable mess with no clear exit.

**Iran Conflict Assessment**
- Iran is unified (no meaningful hardliner/moderate splits), resolute, and using advanced Chinese (and likely Russian) satellite intelligence for precise ballistic missile strikes on escalating target lists.
- Current hits include regional infrastructure (e.g., UAE’s Fujairah oil outlet, Red Sea/Saudi assets via Houthis, Jordan, Kurdish areas); higher-tier economic targets risk global depression by disrupting commodity flows.
- Red Sea is strategically more critical than the Persian Gulf (60% of world commodities transit it/Suez); workarounds via Cape of Good Hope massively raise costs, fueling inflation and accelerating China’s rail alternatives.
- US strategy under Trump/Hegseth is incoherent “wishy-washy” bombing, blockade rhetoric, and denial of setbacks (e.g., Houthi resilience, US personnel in Syria); it doubles down on destruction without diplomacy.
- Gulf states (Kuwait, Bahrain, etc.) are highly vulnerable to collapse, militia advances, or absorption; Iran avoids some strikes to preserve international sympathy (global polls increasingly favor China over the US as a threat).

**Broader Strategic and Nuclear Risks**
- US is being ousted from Southwest Asia after failing to defeat Iran; desperate moves include greenlighting Saudi nuclear enrichment (risking rapid weaponization and regional proliferation, reversing earlier nonproliferation hopes).
- Wilkerson believes Iran may already possess or be ready to use a nuclear weapon (one strike between Tel Aviv and Haifa could devastate Israel); balance favors Iran with Russian/Chinese backing.
- Parallel Russia-NATO war has reached a critical phase: Russia views itself at war with NATO and may strike a NATO country to restore deterrence, with preparations for uncontrolled escalation.
- Hatred (of Iranians, Russians, Muslims) drives policy over strategy or economics; Congress (bipartisan) and media amplify destruction metrics; JCPOA-era progress has been squandered.

**Global Economic and Humanitarian Fallout**
- Disruptions threaten diesel shortages, fertilizer/crop inputs, rare earths/electronics (impacting US defense production), and food supplies—pushing the world toward depression worse than 2008 and starving the Global South.
- Climate stresses plus food crises will drive massive northward migration (armed young men seeking resources), prompting border militarization and conflict.
- Diplomacy has collapsed; leaders act like children, measuring success only in death and ruin; multipolar opportunities are ignored in favor of recreating lost unipolarity.

**US Domestic Politics, Alliances, and Military**
- Trump administration faces rapid poll collapse, midterm wipeout risk, and potential accountability for dereliction; legalizing Israel intelligence-sharing (via NDAA) echoes historical treason (Pollard).
- Parallels to LBJ/Vietnam: domestic politics trap leaders in unwinnable wars; civil-war-like divisions (1859–60 levels) exist but Americans may lack the will for frank confrontation.
- US alliances resemble vassalage; Gulf states and Europe deepen dependence out of fear, yet risk self-destruction—potential for eventual breakaway if national interests demand distance.
- Military culture is corrupted: sycophantic leadership, revolving-door incentives, ethical collapse (e.g., due-process-free killings, ICE militarization); lifelong officers express disbelief and heartbreak at the degradation of the institution they served.
- Overall: empire and republic in profound self-inflicted peril; lack of political imagination may require cataclysm (risking nuclear self-destruction) before new cooperative structures emerge. Both speakers end deeply pessimistic.
