The hosts preview the entire UFC Abu Dhabi card from a betting perspective, repeatedly stressing the uncertainty around regional prospects, short-notice replacements, travel, weight cuts, and judging in Abu Dhabi. Their core approach is to favor proven fighters and stylistic paths—especially durable volume strikers, defensive wrestlers, and fighters facing opponents with cardio or durability concerns—while avoiding oversized money-line favorites unless a finish or round prop offers better value.

**Main event: Magomed Ankalaev vs. Bogdan Guskov**
- Both hosts pick Magomed Ankalaev, despite disliking his roughly -550 price. They believe Guskov’s primary route is an early knockout, citing his power and Jan Błachowicz knockdown, while questioning whether Guskov can sustain a hard five-round fight on short notice.
- They note Ankalaev often makes fights closer than necessary by choosing to strike rather than wrestle, referencing close fights with Aleksandar Rakić, Thiago Santos, and Błachowicz. Still, they expect Ankalaev to take over in rounds three through five if he survives Guskov’s early danger.
- One host likes “fight doesn’t go to decision” at about -400 as a parlay addition, arguing it covers Guskov’s knockout route and a potential late Ankalaev stoppage.

**Steve Erceg vs. Ramazan Temirov**
- The hosts split: Paul leans to Temirov by knockout, at approximately +225 to +250, because he sees Erceg as hittable and vulnerable to Temirov’s heavy hands.
- Cody narrowly picks Steve Erceg based on his higher-level experience, size, reach, volume, ability to work over 15 minutes, and fights against opponents such as Alexandre Pantoja, Kai Kara-France, and Tim Elliott.
- Both agree Temirov is dangerous but low-volume, with limited grappling depth and a likely need for a knockout. They raise uncertainty around Temirov’s return from a 12-month suspension involving banned heart medication and consider weigh-ins important for assessing Erceg’s travel and cut.

**Islam Dulatov vs. Wellington Turman**
- Both strongly favor Islam Dulatov, though they are reluctant to use his -1,000 range money line heavily. They see Dulatov’s best paths as a first-round knockout, submission after hurting Turman, or the under 1.5 rounds.
- They describe Dulatov as a 27-year-old German celebrity with sharp, violent striking, clinch elbows, and improving counter-grappling, but acknowledge he remains insufficiently tested beyond the first round.
- Turman has not fought in roughly two and a half years because of a shoulder injury. The hosts cite losses and struggles after moving to welterweight, including Randy Brown and Jared Gooden, and believe the 170-pound cut and trip to Abu Dhabi could worsen his cardio.
- They concede Turman’s theoretical route is to use his Glover Teixeira/Alex Pereira training, clinch Dulatov, wrestle, and seek a submission, but find little evidence that he can execute it.

**Muslim Salikhov? / “Muhammad Zanakov” vs. Damian Zięba?**
- The discussion concerns a favored Dagestani striker, referred to in the captions as “Muhammad Zanakov,” against unbeaten Polish prospect Damian “Zepki/Zapki.” Cody picks the favorite, while Paul bets the underdog.
- Cody expects the favorite’s constant pace, Muay Thai combinations, leg kicks, cardio, and defensive wrestling to break the newcomer over 15 minutes. He views the Contender Series veteran as a potentially strong UFC addition, albeit reckless and vulnerable to counters.
- Paul argues the approximately -300/+250 line is badly skewed. He likes the Polish fighter’s movement, stance-switching, body work, youth—24 versus the favorite’s reported 31—and training with Ilia Topuria’s team.
- Paul has already backed the underdog around +293 and also played a decision prop around +800. He accepts that the newcomer is a finisher and that a decision would depend on favorable judging, but believes the matchup should be near a pick’em.

**Rizvan Kuniev vs. Tyrell Fortune**
- Both hosts choose Rizvan Kuniev over former Bellator heavyweight Tyrell Fortune. Paul has Kuniev in parlays and likes Kuniev by decision around +150.
- Their case centers on Kuniev’s defensive wrestling: they cite a 93% UFC takedown-defense figure, Curtis Blaydes going 2-for-15 on takedowns, and Jailton Almeida going 0-for-8. They expect Fortune’s wrestling attempts to tire him out.
- Fortune, now 36, is credited for beating Marcin Tybura by controlling position and landing necessary takedowns, but the hosts see recurring cardio, durability, and late-fight issues in his record.
- They expect Kuniev to be the better striker if the fight remains standing and to win later rounds or a decision if he stuffs Fortune’s wrestling.

**Saigid Izagakhmaev vs. Abubakar Vagaev**
- Both select Abubakar Vagaev, but emphasize his vulnerability to being knocked out despite being technically superior. They cite three knockout losses in which he was fully unconscious, while noting he won decision rematches over Beslan Ushukov and Murad Abdulaev.
- They characterize Vagaev as a sophisticated defensive wrestler and clean technical boxer who usually wins decisions rather than finishes. His preferred style is to defend takedowns, remain upright, and accumulate sharp basic combinations.
- Saigid is criticized for a poor showing against Nicolas Dalby: the hosts say he went 4-for-10 on takedowns, produced only about 20 significant strikes, tired badly, and failed to control positions.
- Their preferred betting angles are over 2.5 rounds, fight to go the distance, or Vagaev by decision, rather than Vagaev’s approximately -265 money line. They believe Saigid’s main upset chance is catching Vagaev with a single large shot.

**Valter Walker vs. Thomas Petersen**
- Paul takes a small shot on Valter Walker by knockout at +615, reasoning that Petersen’s wrestling may prevent Walker from easily setting up his familiar leg-lock game and force a sloppy heavyweight striking exchange.
- Cody favors Walker by submission despite believing the market may overprice it after four straight heel-hook wins. He argues Walker does not always need a clean takedown; he can force a scramble, fall back on a leg, and create heel-hook opportunities.
- Petersen is described as a junior-college national wrestling champion with 87% takedown defense, but also as a fighter who makes tactical mistakes, tires, and has been caught in bad situations.
- Both see it as a useful live-betting fight: Walker is especially dangerous in the opening minutes, but Petersen becomes more appealing if he stuffs early attempts and reaches the second round.

**Dustin Jacoby vs. Muhammad “Sai/Sed”**
- Both clearly pick Dustin Jacoby, with Paul using him in a parlay. They do not understand market support for Muhammad Sai, a short-notice UFC debutant whose earlier footage has reportedly been removed and whose visible opposition appears weak.
- They view this as likely a striking matchup and favor Jacoby’s Glory kickboxing background, UFC experience, cardio, range, leg kicks, and proven ability to sustain a hard 15 minutes.
- The hosts acknowledge Jacoby can be knocked out against elite opposition, but see no tape-based reason to classify Sai alongside opponents such as Dominick Reyes or Khalil Rountree. They expect Jacoby to be the more polished and proven striker.

**Sam Patterson vs. Santiago Ponzinibbio**
- Both select Sam Patterson, primarily as a parlay piece at about -400, over 39-year-old Santiago Ponzinibbio, who is returning after roughly 16 months away and taking the fight on short notice.
- They believe Patterson’s old “chinny” reputation was largely shaped by his knockout loss at lightweight. At welterweight, they see his durability as improved, citing his response to a clean Danny Barlow shot.
- Patterson is described as 6-foot-3 with a 78-inch reach, a BJJ black belt, improving boxing, and a physical advantage at 170 pounds. They expect him either to exploit Ponzinibbio on the ground or keep him at range and land damaging straight shots.
- They avoid forcing a round-one or under-1.5 bet despite considering a Patterson finish plausible, because they can also envision a more measured fight reaching later rounds.

**Ismael Bonfim vs. Axel Sola**
- Both pick Axel Sola, though Paul’s actual wager is under 2.5 rounds at about -120. They see Bonfim as dangerous early but unreliable once faced with resistance.
- Cody calls Bonfim a highly talented but fragile “glass cannon”: explosive, powerful, and athletic, but compromised by difficult lightweight cuts, weak takedown defense, fatigue, and a tendency to unravel when opponents return fire or establish top control.
- They point to Bonfim’s missed weights, his loss to Benoît Saint Denis, the stoppage after a head kick from Nazim Sadykhov, and Chris Padilla’s ability to break him down.
- Sola is considered defensively porous and prone to absorbing damage, as seen against Mason Jones, but durable, physically strong, and determined. The hosts believe that if Sola survives the first round, he should take over through pressure, grappling, and Bonfim’s cardio decline.

**Magomed Tukhov/Tukulov vs. Brendson Ribeiro**
- Both pick the large favorite, Magomed Tukhov, but do not see value at -800 or higher. They regard him as a legitimate but still unproven prospect with crisp straight punches, accurate kicking, size, and a notable first-round knockout of Caio Machado.
- Their caution is based on Tukhov’s limited quality of opposition and unanswered questions about his takedown defense, submission defense, and performance in longer fights.
- Brendson Ribeiro is credited as a large, athletic fighter with submission ability and occasional explosive offense, but the hosts view him as another fighter who struggles when adversity arrives.
- They expect Tukhov’s precision and power to eventually overwhelm Ribeiro, likely by knockout, but prefer more moderately priced favorites such as Patterson, Kuniev, or Jacoby for parlays.

**Nursulton Ruziboev? / “Nurullo Aliev” vs. Mike Davis**
- The hosts treat this as a close and uncomfortable fight. Cody picks Mike Davis as an underdog; Paul calls it “dog or pass,” waiting in hopes Davis’s price rises above +195.
- Their concern with backing Davis is his prior Abu Dhabi performance against Fares Ziam, which they attribute—without certainty—to reported paint-fume issues in the locker room. They say he looked unlike himself and made strange choices, including pulling guard against a striker.
- They criticize Nurullo Aliev’s low-volume, close-decision style despite his youth and wrestling. They cite narrow or underwhelming decisions over Rafael Alves, Joe Solecki, and Yanal Ashmouz, arguing he has not clearly progressed and lands under three significant strikes per minute.
- Davis is seen as the substantially better striker with more pace, power, and scrambling ability. However, Aliev could win a close Abu Dhabi decision through takedowns and control time. Both also like distance-related angles: over 2.5 rounds around -210 or fight to go the distance around -170.

**Abdul Hussein vs. Cody Gibson**
- Both strongly favor Abdul Hussein over Cody Gibson, with Paul initially playing early unders. They describe Hussein as a real prospect with elite pace and wrestling, improving boxing, strong regional experience, and amateur credentials including the 2015 IMMAF European title and 2016 IMMAF world title.
- They cite Hussein’s competitive loss to Muhammad Mokaev and wins over fighters including Sammy Yaya, Tyson Nam, and Sylvester Chipfumbu as evidence that he has faced meaningful regional competition.
- Gibson, 38, enters on short notice. The hosts argue his durability and cardio are declining, referencing his quick knockout loss to Rani Yahya?—captioned as “Richie Lang”—and his second-round fade and submission loss to Da’Mon Blackshear.
- Hussein is smaller than Gibson but expected to be quicker, fresher, harder to hold down, and more capable in scrambles. They consider the -500 range fair and see Gibson as a veteran moving in the wrong direction.

**Final betting slate and conclusion**
- Cody’s preferred “PRP” picks are: Magomed Ankalaev, Steve Erceg, Islam Dulatov, the favored Dagestani striker in the Zięba matchup, Rizvan Kuniev, Abubakar Vagaev, Valter Walker, Dustin Jacoby, Sam Patterson, Axel Sola, Magomed Tukhov, Mike Davis, and Abdul Hussein.
- Paul’s highlighted parlay consists of Kuniev, Jacoby, and Patterson. He intentionally avoids stacking huge favorites such as Dulatov or Tukhov because they add little return while retaining MMA upset risk.
- The hosts conclude that the card contains several difficult-to-price regional prospects and many uncertain favorites. They recommend limiting exposure, checking weigh-ins, being cautious with Middle East cards and close decisions, and considering live betting where early-versus-late dynamics are pronounced.
