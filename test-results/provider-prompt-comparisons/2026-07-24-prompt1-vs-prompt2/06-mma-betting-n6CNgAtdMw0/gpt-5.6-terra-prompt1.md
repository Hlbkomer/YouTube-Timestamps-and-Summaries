This is a betting breakdown for UFC Abu Dhabi. The hosts analyze each matchup, focusing on fighter styles, durability, cardio, experience, regional-scene records, and betting value. Their main theme is caution around several heavily favored local or undefeated prospects, whose odds may be inflated despite limited proven competition.

**Main Event: Magomed Ankalaev vs. Bogdan Guskov**
- Both pick Ankalaev to win, likely taking over in the later rounds of the five-round fight.
- Guskov’s main chance is an early knockout; he has legitimate power but questionable cardio, grappling, and ability to sustain five rounds on short notice.
- They do not like Ankalaev’s steep -550 price because he often fights cautiously, leaves close decisions to judges, and does not consistently use his wrestling.
- Preferred angle: the fight not to go the distance, since Guskov is unlikely to win a long fight and may fade badly.

**Key Betting Picks**
- The hosts’ main parlay includes:
  - Rizvan Kuniev
  - Dustin Jacoby
  - Sam Patterson
- Cody’s final preferred picks/“PRP” also include Ankalaev, Steve Erceg, Islam Dulatov, Magomed Zaynukov, Abubakar Vagaev, Walter Walker, Axel Sola, Magomed Tukhov, Mike Davis, and Abdul Hussein.
- They stress waiting for weigh-ins and limiting exposure due to the uncertainty of many prospects and the volatility of MMA.

**Steve Erceg vs. Ramazan Temirov**
- Split opinion:
  - Paul likes Temirov by knockout at plus money, believing Erceg can be hurt and Temirov has major power.
  - Cody picks Erceg due to his experience, size, reach, volume, and ability to win a decision if he avoids a knockout.
- Temirov is coming off a suspension related to heart medication, creating uncertainty about his conditioning and performance.
- Consensus: Temirov likely needs a knockout; Erceg likely wins through activity and scorecards.

**Islam Dulatov vs. Wellington Turman**
- Strong agreement on Dulatov, especially by first-round finish.
- Turman has been inactive for over two years, has dealt with shoulder issues, struggled since dropping to welterweight, and has shown poor cardio.
- Dulatov is an explosive striker with improving counter-grappling, but his true ceiling remains unclear because he has faced soft opposition.
- They dislike using Dulatov at an enormous moneyline price, preferring first-round, knockout, or under-round props.

**Magomed Zaynukov vs. Damian Zepki**
- Major disagreement:
  - Cody picks Zaynukov due to his relentless pace, durable style, advanced striking, cardio, and defensive wrestling.
  - Paul strongly backs underdog Zepki, believing the line is too wide and that Zepki’s movement, body work, age, and technical striking make the matchup closer to a pick’em.
- Paul bets Zepki’s moneyline and a long-shot decision prop.
- Main concern for Zaynukov: he can be wild, reckless, and has been knocked down against lower-level opposition.

**Rizvan Kuniev vs. Tyrell Fortune**
- Both favor Kuniev, with interest in Kuniev by decision.
- Fortune has wrestling ability but has shown poor cardio, fading late in several fights, and is now 36.
- Kuniev has excellent takedown defense, including strong performances against high-level wrestlers Curtis Blaydes and Jailton Almeida.
- Expectation: Fortune may attempt wrestling early, fail to maintain it, tire out, and lose a decision or late finish.

**Abubakar Vagaev vs. Saeid**
- Both pick Vagaev, but warn that his chin is a recurring concern.
- Vagaev is viewed as a highly technical, well-rounded fighter with strong defensive wrestling and clean boxing, but all of his recent losses came by decisive knockout.
- Saeid struggled badly with Nicolas Dalby and may not have the wrestling or cardio to impose his game.
- Preferred betting angles: over 2.5 rounds, fight goes the distance, or Vagaev by decision rather than laying a large moneyline price.

**Walter Walker vs. Thomas Petersen**
- Walker has won four straight by heel hook, making his submission prop an obvious public betting angle.
- Paul is skeptical Walker can consistently take down or submit Petersen, a strong wrestler with solid takedown defense, and takes a small Walker knockout prop instead.
- Cody still favors Walker by submission, arguing he creates scrambles and falls back on leg locks rather than relying on traditional takedowns.
- Both identify this as a possible live-betting fight:
  - Walker is dangerous early.
  - If Petersen survives the first round and stuffs takedowns, he could take over later.

**Dustin Jacoby vs. Muhammad Sayed**
- Both strongly favor Jacoby.
- Sayed is an undefeated short-notice UFC newcomer, but much of his footage and competition level are questionable.
- Jacoby has a major advantage in striking experience, cardio, kickboxing pedigree, and proven UFC-level competition.
- The hosts think market support for Sayed is hard to justify based on the available tape.

**Sam Patterson vs. Santiago Ponzinibbio**
- Both favor Patterson, mainly as a parlay piece.
- Ponzinibbio is 39, has been inactive for 16 months, is taking the bout on short notice, and has shown declining speed and durability.
- Patterson has improved since moving from lightweight to welterweight, where his previous “chinny” reputation appears less relevant.
- They expect Patterson’s size, reach, boxing, and grappling to give him multiple paths to win, potentially by late stoppage.

**Ismael Bonfim vs. Axel Sola**
- Both pick Sola, though Paul bets under 2.5 rounds rather than the side.
- Bonfim is talented, fast, and dangerous early, but has struggled with weight cuts, adversity, wrestling defense, and late-fight cardio.
- Sola absorbs too much damage and has defensive flaws, but is tough, physically strong, and more likely to persist if the fight goes past the opening round.
- They see Bonfim as dangerous early but likely to fade if he cannot finish Sola quickly.

**Magomed Tukhov vs. Brenson Ribeiro**
- Both pick Tukhov to win by knockout, but advise against laying his massive -800-type price.
- Tukhov is an undefeated prospect with sharp, accurate striking and a strong amateur background, but his UFC-level ceiling remains uncertain due to weak past opposition.
- Ribeiro is athletic, large, and dangerous offensively, but tends to collapse when opponents withstand his early attack or apply pressure.
- Tukhov’s win over Kyle Machado is cited as evidence of his finishing ability and potential.

**Nursulton Ruziboev / Naurullo Aliyev vs. Mike Davis**
- Cody picks Mike Davis as an underdog; Paul calls it “dog or pass” and is waiting for a better price.
- Aliyev is a low-volume wrestler who often wins close, unspectacular decisions and has not shown much finishing ability.
- Davis is considered the superior striker with the tools to defend takedowns and scramble back up, but his prior Abu Dhabi performance was unusually poor amid alleged paint-fume issues backstage.
- Both note that judging and control time could favor Aliyev in a close fight in Abu Dhabi.
- They also like over-rounds/fight-goes-distance angles due to both fighters’ limited finishing profiles.

**Abdul Hussein vs. Cody Gibson**
- Both strongly favor Hussein, with early-under bets also mentioned.
- Hussein is seen as a legitimate UFC-ready prospect: a high-level wrestler with excellent pace, cardio, scrambling, and improving boxing.
- Gibson, now 38, is viewed as declining in durability and conditioning after being knocked out by Ritchie Lang and submitted by Da’Mon Blackshear.
- Hussein has proven himself against strong regional competition, while Gibson is taking a difficult short-notice trip and appears to be heading in the wrong direction.

**Overall Takeaways**
- The card has many entertaining matchups and likely violence, but also several uncertain regional prospects with inflated odds.
- Avoid blindly betting huge favorites simply because they are undefeated, local, or associated with prominent MMA regions.
- The hosts favor selective parlays, decision/finish props, totals, and live-betting opportunities over repeatedly laying extreme moneyline prices.
