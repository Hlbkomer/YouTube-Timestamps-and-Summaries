The reviewer gives Christopher Nolan’s *The Odyssey* a largely negative, indifferent verdict: despite its ambition, technical craft, and major cast, they found it emotionally cold, visually bleak, and ultimately boring. They argue that Nolan’s grounded treatment strips Homer’s story of its mythic wonder, humanity, and catharsis.

**Main Topic**
- The film is described as one of 2026’s most divisive releases, surrounded by intense praise, backlash, and culture-war controversy.
- The reviewer rejects both extremes, calling it neither a disaster nor a masterpiece—just “meh.”
- Their central criticism is that the movie is technically impressive but fails to create emotional investment.

**Story Overview**
- The film follows Odysseus, played by Matt Damon, after the Trojan War as he struggles through the long journey back to Ithaca.
- Its story is framed through flashbacks, covering major episodes from the myth:
  - The Trojan Horse and fall of Troy
  - The Cyclops and Poseidon’s anger
  - Circe turning men into pigs
  - A journey to the underworld
  - The forbidden cattle, shipwreck, and years spent with Calypso
  - Odysseus’s return to Penelope and the suitors in Ithaca

**Criticism of Nolan’s Approach**
- The reviewer believes Nolan’s commitment to realism and groundedness undermines the magical, mystical, and adventurous qualities of *The Odyssey*.
- They describe the film as:
  - Bleak, muted, and visually dreary
  - Slow and lacking urgency
  - Precise and intelligent, but without romance, wonder, or emotional warmth
- In their view, the film turns an epic myth into something clinical and detached.

**Characters and Performances**
- Odysseus is criticized as cold, distant, and insufficiently motivated by his supposed desire to return to his wife, son, and kingdom.
- The reviewer feels the character is portrayed more as a traumatized veteran passively enduring punishment than as the clever, determined, adventurous hero of the original myth.
- The climactic reunion with his family and revenge against the suitors is said to feel flat and procedural rather than triumphant or cathartic.
- Robert Pattinson’s antagonist is singled out as the only performance that strongly provokes an emotional reaction.
- The reviewer also mocks certain casting choices, including John Leguizamo in an aged-servant role.

**Casting Controversy**
- The video addresses controversy over the casting of Elliot Page, Lupita Nyong’o, and Zendaya.
- The reviewer says these actors have limited screen time and do not significantly affect the plot or their overall opinion of the movie.
- They acknowledge that some viewers may object on principle, but argue the controversy is disproportionate to the characters’ actual importance.

**Dialogue and Tone**
- The reviewer strongly dislikes the dialogue, calling it blunt, clumsy, overly modern, and full of jarring slang and profanity.
- They also criticize the use of American accents and language they feel does not fit the ancient Greek setting.

**Technical Strengths**
- Nolan is praised for practical effects, real locations, and the ambition of the production.
- However, the reviewer feels the locations do not resemble the Eastern Mediterranean; the washed-out color palette, gray skies, and windswept scenery make it feel more like “Scotland in November.”

**Final Verdict**
- The reviewer considers *The Odyssey* an impressive and thought-provoking production that will likely earn major money and awards.
- However, they found it emotionally sterile and forgettable—a grand adventure that lacks spirit, a hero’s journey without a compelling hero, and an epic without genuine scale or wonder.
