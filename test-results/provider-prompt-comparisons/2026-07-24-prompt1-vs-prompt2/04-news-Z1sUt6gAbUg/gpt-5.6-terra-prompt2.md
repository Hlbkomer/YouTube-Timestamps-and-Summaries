President Trump reiterates that Iran must not obtain a nuclear weapon and suggests Tehran is being hit hard but is not yet prepared to make a durable deal. United Against Nuclear Iran CEO Mark Wallace argues that the alleged Pickaxe Mountain site should be neutralized because, in his view, its depth and construction point to a nuclear purpose.

**Pickaxe Mountain concerns**
- Wallace calls Pickaxe Mountain a deeply buried site “clouded in mystery,” with disagreement over whether it is currently operational or planned for future nuclear activity.
- He says there has been reliable reporting that Iran moved centrifuges and other technical nuclear capabilities there before “Midnight Hammer” last year, though accounts differ on whether the facility was active or whether equipment was being preserved from bombing.
- Wallace also cites suspicious satellite-imagery activity around the mountain.

**Military options**
- Wallace is unsure that the site is beyond the reach of U.S. bunker-busting weapons, noting that deep-penetrating munitions have previously been used against buried facilities.
- Rather than deploying ground forces, he says the United States could potentially seal or bury the facility, preventing Iran from accessing any nuclear materials or infrastructure there.

**Wallace’s assessment of Iran**
- Wallace says he does not believe the current Iranian regime will abandon its nuclear ambitions.
- He characterizes Iran’s nuclear program as part of a broader strategy to gain leverage through regional proxy groups, alleged assassination threats, and threats to disrupt the Bab al-Mandab and Strait of Hormuz.
- He argues that Iran’s ideology and threats toward the United States, Israel, Saudi Arabia, and neighboring states make its potential acquisition of a nuclear weapon especially dangerous.
