The host’s central view is that Bitcoin remains in a short- to medium-term bearish phase after failing to reclaim key levels, while traditional equities and many momentum stocks may be due for short-term pullbacks despite still-bullish longer-term structures. He expects Bitcoin to test roughly the low-$70,000s or upper-$60,000s, but does not expect a sustained break to new cycle lows because higher-timeframe monthly momentum is near areas that have previously marked major lows.

**Traditional markets: likely pullbacks, not a macro top yet**
- The host said the Nasdaq reached his 30,000 target sooner than expected. Bearish RSI divergence could produce a pullback toward roughly 29,000, particularly if it closes below the prior day’s low, but he still views dips in traditional markets as buying opportunities through year-end.
- For SPY, he identified 7,513 as a near-term trigger: a close below it could lead toward roughly 7,450–7,420. Still, he does not expect a major market top until closer to Q4.
- Across semiconductors and high-momentum equities, he repeatedly warned that many rallies look “exaggerated” and advised taking partial profits rather than trying to hold every move indefinitely.

**Bitcoin: bearish bias below key resistance**
- Bitcoin’s CME price remains below the RevRibbons mid-band, which the host placed around $78,800. He said that as long as Bitcoin stays below that level, he has “no reason to be bullish.”
- He cited hidden bearish divergence across numerous timeframes—four-hour through weekly—along with weakening momentum indicators and positive funding rates as evidence favoring a downside continuation.
- Bitcoin was holding near the six-hour 200-day simple moving average around $75,000. The host expects that level to break, with:
  - About $74,000 as an initial Fibonacci area.
  - Roughly $71,300–$71,500 as the 50% retracement and a likely bounce-attempt zone.
  - Around $68,000–$68,500 as a more important downside target, supported by a head-and-shoulders pattern and the 61.8% retracement.
- He said Bitcoin reclaiming the prior day’s high would materially improve the short- and medium-term picture, potentially opening a move back toward $80,000–$81,000. Until then, he considers downside more probable.
- While bearish in the near term, he does not expect large timeframes to close decisively below prior lows. He said he plans to buy in the low-$70,000s and upper-$60,000s, while acknowledging those entries could temporarily go underwater.

**Quantum-computing risk to Bitcoin**
- The host acknowledged that quantum computing is a real long-term threat to Bitcoin’s cryptography. However, he considers the narrative overstated because a quantum computer capable of breaking Bitcoin could also target much larger pools of wealth in banking and financial systems.
- He believes Bitcoin developers are aware of the issue and likely to pursue solutions before it becomes decisive. He said diversification remains his practical response rather than panic.

**Trading and options guidance**
- The host stressed that investors should define invalidation levels and risk before entering trades, take profits during extreme rallies, and avoid “full-porting” into any asset.
- He said he does not trade every directional view: believing an asset may fall does not automatically mean he will short it. He prefers focusing on one asset at a time and said Bitcoin options largely occupy his attention.
- Discussing MicroStrategy, he suggested possible call-credit spreads such as 190/200 or, for shorter horizons, 170/175–180, and said a put spread around 130/100 could be one bearish approach.
- He strongly criticized buying naked, far-out-of-the-money December calls on Hive when implied volatility is already high. He argued that someone bullish on the stock would often be better served by owning the underlying shares than by buying expensive out-of-the-money premium.
- On portfolio education, he said his planned long-term material will cover asset-allocation principles but cannot prescribe a universal dollar amount or position size, since age, risk tolerance, goals, and circumstances differ.

**Closing market view**
- The host concluded that the near-term setup favors a broad pullback: SPY and Nasdaq appeared to be forming local highs, Bitcoin had fallen below $75,000 and could reach about $70,000 or lower, and many individual equities were stretched.
- He framed that weakness as an opportunity for disciplined traders and investors rather than a reason for indiscriminate pessimism, emphasizing predefined risk levels, patience, and partial profit-taking.
